Horizontal (anterior–posterior) GRF

Show the horizontal GRF panel
The horizontal component of GRF occurs because your center of mass is only directly above your point of ground contact for a single instant, near midstance. Before that instant, the horizontal force is posterior — a braking force. After it, the horizontal force is anterior — a propulsive force.

Discussion prompts

When is vGRF equal to, above, and below bodyweight?
Equal to BW: at the four points where the curve crosses the dashed 1.0 BW line — rising early in loading response, falling into the midstance dip, rising again out of the dip into propulsion, and falling late in terminal stance as pushoff eases.
Above BW: during the two peaks — load acceptance (B) and propulsion (C) — while the body is actively accelerating its center of mass upward.
Below BW: through midstance, while the center of mass is near the top of its rise-and-fall path and briefly decelerating vertically.
What's the difference between average and instantaneous loading rate?
Average loading rate is the overall slope of the vGRF curve across the whole load acceptance phase (from initial contact to the B peak).
Instantaneous loading rate is the single steepest slope reached at any moment within that phase — often what's flagged as clinically relevant in injury mechanics (e.g., stress fractures, joint loading).
