KINE 3620 · One tab per lecture. Signs follow our class system: +x right, +y anterior, +z superior, and the right-hand rule. Hip flexion +, knee flexion − (extension +), ankle dorsiflexion +. Angles are in radians.

Muscle architecture

What this is for: see how pennation angle changes the force that reaches the tendon (F·cos α) and why longer fascicles shorten faster.

Balance and the external moment arm

What this is for: lean an inverted-pendulum body and watch gravity’s external moment grow with COM height and lean, and see when the COM leaves the base of support.

The walking gait cycle

What this is for: name the phases and events of one stride, read the vertical GRF through stance, and time real gait events from video. All three tools use the same phase scheme.

Gait kinematics: joint angles from video

What this is for: place three points (proximal, joint center, distal) on a video frame and get the joint angle in our sign convention, e.g. knee −1.05 rad (−60°, flexion).

Moments and moment arms

What this is for: change the elbow angle and watch the muscle’s and the forearm weight’s moment arms change, then compare the internal and external moments (τ = F × d⊥). Window 3, Knee and the GRF: set the GRF’s size, angle from the ground and perpendicular distance from the knee, then read the external knee moment (GRF in front = extension +, behind = flexion −) and the equal and opposite internal moment needed.

Walking vs running

What this is for: compare double support with the float phase, and the walking and running vGRF curves (impact peak, active peak, loading rate).

Sprinting kinetics

What this is for: follow force and power across a race, apply net impulse = change in momentum to one ground contact, and compare leg and vertical stiffness.
