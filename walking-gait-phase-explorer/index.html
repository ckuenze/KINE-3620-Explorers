Walking Gait Phase Explorer

Scrub through one walking gait cycle of the right limb, from right initial contact to the next right initial contact. Name the phase and sub-phase at any point.

Pre-class: work alone through the guided steps, then answer the check questions for the reading's "Before class" list.

In-class: partner activity. Predict first, test it with the tool, then talk through the discussion questions.

Pre-class: guided steps

  1. Press Play and watch one full cycle. Stance runs from initial contact (0%) to toe off (60%).
  2. Use Next sub-phase to step through all seven sub-phases. Read each description.
  3. Drag the slider to 5% and to 55%. Both feet are on the ground: that is double support.
  4. Read the quick reference at the bottom: step, stride, and cadence.

Check yourself

At 45% of the cycle, is the limb in stance or swing, and which sub-phase?

Stance, terminal stance (30–50%). The heel is lifting off while the body moves ahead of the foot.

At 80% of the cycle?

Swing, mid swing (73–87%). The limb passes under the body.

What is the difference between a step and a stride?

A step runs from one foot's contact to the other foot's contact. A stride is one full cycle of one limb, so one stride = two steps.

Cadence is 110 steps/min and step length is 0.70 m. What is walking speed?

Step frequency = 110 ÷ 60 = 1.83 steps/s. Speed = step length × step frequency = 0.70 m × 1.83 steps/s = 1.28 m/s. Stride length = 2 × 0.70 = 1.40 m.

In-class: partner challenge

  1. Predict: is the whole-body COM highest at toe off or at midstance? Then test: scrub and watch the COM.
  2. Predict: which sub-phases are double support? Then test: check the support line under the sub-phase name.
  3. Quiz your partner: turn on Hide names. One partner sets a %, the other names the phase and sub-phase. Then show the names and check.

Discuss

  • Initial contact and toe off are instants, not phases. Why do we treat them as events?
  • If someone walks faster, what do you expect to happen to the share of the cycle spent in double support?
  • The right leg's pre-swing overlaps with which sub-phase of the left leg?

Quick reference
Step length
Distance from one foot's contact to the other foot's next contact.
Stride length
Distance covered in one full cycle of one limb: two steps.
Cadence
Steps per minute. Step frequency = cadence ÷ 60 (steps/s).
Speed
Speed = step length × step frequency (m × steps/s = m/s).

Sub-phase boundaries are typical textbook (Rancho Los Amigos style) values for walking at a comfortable speed, not measured data.
