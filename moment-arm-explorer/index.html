The weight's moment arm is the horizontal distance to the center of mass — that works because gravity's line of action is always vertical. The muscle's moment arm isn't a horizontal (or any straight-line) distance to the insertion point — it's the perpendicular distance to the muscle's whole line of pull, which real tendons bend around the joint. That's a wrapping effect a simple diagram can't draw exactly, so the muscle curve here follows a typical illustrative pattern — smallest at the extremes of the joint's range, largest somewhere in the middle — rather than being measured from the insertion point shown. Real muscle moment-arm curves vary by muscle and by person.
