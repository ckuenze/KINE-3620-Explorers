Moment Arm Explorer

Windows 1–2: one elbow angle changes two moment arms. Window 3: the ground reaction force (GRF) makes an external moment about the knee. τ = F × d⊥. Joint convention, right-hand rule about +x, person faces right (+y): elbow flexion +, knee extension +.

Pre-class: guided steps

  1. Leave the forces alone. Move θ from 0 to 2.62 rad and watch both moment arms in Window 1.
  2. Find the angle where the weight's moment arm is largest. Then find where the muscle's is largest.
  3. In Window 2, check one torque by hand: τ = F × d⊥, using the numbers shown.
  4. Read the verdict. Note the sign of the net moment and which way the forearm accelerates.

Window 3: Knee and the GRF

  1. Start at the defaults (800 N, 1.57 rad (90°), 0.050 m in front of the knee). Check the external knee moment by hand: M = F × d⊥.
  2. Press Behind the knee. Watch the sign of the external moment and of the internal moment needed both flip.
  3. Set d⊥ to 0.000 m. What moment does the GRF make about the knee now?
  4. Change the GRF angle. The components Fy and Fz change, but the moment only depends on F and d⊥.

Check yourself

What is the torque equation, and what are its units?

τ = F × d⊥: force times the perpendicular distance from the joint axis to the force's line of action. Units: N·m.

Internal vs external moment: what is the difference?

An internal moment comes from structures inside the body (here, the elbow flexors). An external moment comes from forces outside the body (here, the forearm's weight). At the elbow, the muscle's moment is + (flexion) and the weight's is − (extension).

Window 3: a 900 N GRF passes 0.040 m behind the knee. What are the external and internal knee moments?

External: −900 N × 0.040 m = −36.00 N·m, an external flexion moment (knee extension is +). The internal moment needed is equal and opposite, +36.00 N·m: an internal extension moment from the quadriceps.

Window 3: when in walking stance does the GRF usually pass in front of the knee?

In terminal stance. The GRF in front of the knee makes an external extension moment (+), so the internal moment is a flexion moment (−). In loading response the GRF passes behind the knee (external flexion moment, quadriceps supply an internal extension moment).

Why does a moment arm depend on joint angle?

The perpendicular distance from the joint to the line of action changes as the segment rotates. Gravity always acts straight down, so the weight's moment arm is 0.20 m × sin θ: zero hanging straight down, largest with the forearm horizontal (1.57 rad).

  1. Predict: with the muscle force held at 350 N, at which angle is the net flexion moment largest? Then test by sweeping θ.
  2. Predict: at θ = 1.57 rad and 40 N segment weight, how much muscle force makes the net moment zero? Solve F = W·d ÷ dₘ first. Then test with the "Force to hold" readout.
  3. Predict: if the weight's torque is larger than the muscle's, does the elbow have to be extending? Then discuss using the verdict wording.

Window 3: Knee and the GRF

  1. Predict: in loading response a 760 N GRF passes 0.060 m behind the knee. Sign and size of the external knee moment? Of the internal moment needed? Which muscle group? Then test.
  2. Predict: which raises the knee moment more, doubling F or doubling d⊥? Then test both from 800 N and 0.050 m.

Discuss

  • Why is the same muscle force "strong" at one angle and "weak" at another?

Window 1: how the moment arms change with angle

No force here, only geometry. Watch how differently the two moment arms respond to the same angle change. Moment arms are shown in metres to 3 decimals, because the muscle's changes by only millimetres.

External (weight) moment arm, d (m)

d = 0.20 m × sin θ = 0.142 m

Internal (muscle) moment arm, dₘ (m)

dₘ = 0.025 m

The weight's moment arm is the horizontal distance from the elbow to the forearm's center of mass (0.20 m from the elbow). That works because gravity's line of action is always vertical. The muscle's moment arm is the perpendicular distance from the elbow to the muscle's whole line of pull, which real tendons bend around the joint. So the muscle curve here is an illustrative pattern: smallest at the ends of the range and largest near 1.57 rad (90°), where elbow flexor moment arms are usually drawn as peaking (approximate; real curves vary by muscle and by person).

Window 2: how torque changes with angle (forces held constant)

τ = F × d⊥. The muscle force and the forearm weight are the same at every angle on this chart. Only the moment arms from Window 1 change, which is why these curves aren't flat. Both curves show torque size; the muscle's torque is + (flexion) and the weight's is − (extension).

Window 3: Knee and the GRF

The GRF acts on the foot, but its line of action passes near the knee. Its perpendicular distance from the knee (d⊥) sets the external knee moment: M = F × d⊥. Joint convention: knee extension +. GRF in front of the knee = external extension moment (+); behind = external flexion moment (−). The knee muscles supply an equal and opposite internal moment.
