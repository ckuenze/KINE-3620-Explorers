KINE 3620 · One tab per lecture. Signs follow our class system: +x right, +y anterior, +z superior, and the right-hand rule. Hip flexion +, knee flexion − (extension +), ankle dorsiflexion +, shoulder external rotation +. Angles are in radians, with degrees in parentheses.

The countermovement jump

What this is for: read a force-plate vGRF curve phase by phase (quiet stance, unweighting, braking, propulsion, flight), then get takeoff velocity and jump height two ways: from net impulse (J = Δp) and from net work (= ΔKE).

Jump landing

What this is for: see how fast the body hits the ground, how high the peak force and loading rate get, and how a longer stopping distance lowers the average force. The drop-height model is a model, not data.

Reaction time and agility

What this is for: measure simple and choice reaction time, see Hick’s law (more choices, more time, but by less each time choices double), and feel what a cue or a fake does to your response.

Pitching phases and the kinetic chain

What this is for: step through the six phases and key events of a pitch, compare baseball and windmill softball, and see proximal-to-distal sequencing and arm loading. Angles are rad (deg).

Release speed, angle, and height

What this is for: change release speed, angle, and height and see where the ball lands, why the best angle is below 0.79 rad (45°) for real throwers, and how two angles can hit the same target.
