# Countermovement Jump Explorer

Explore how vertical ground reaction force and center-of-mass (COM) position, velocity, and acceleration change through one countermovement jump. You can also measure your own jump height from a video.

- **Topic:** CMJ kinetics, the impulse–momentum theorem, work, and jump height
- **Use:** In-class
- **Unit:** Unit 3: Movement and Human Performance

## Learning objectives

After using this tool, students should be able to:

- Name the CMJ phases (quiet stance, unweighting, braking, propulsion, flight, landing) and find them on force and COM traces.
- Relate vGRF to COM acceleration with a = (vGRF − BW) ÷ m.
- Use the impulse–momentum theorem to explain where takeoff velocity comes from.
- Read a force vs. position plot and tell net work on the COM apart from work done by the ground force alone.
- Calculate jump height with the takeoff velocity method (v² ÷ 2g) and the flight time method (g × t² ÷ 8), and explain when the two disagree.

## How to use it

Open `index.html` in a web browser. The tool has two modes.

**Explore the jump**
- Use the slider, the **Play** button, or the **Jump to** buttons to move through the jump.
- Switch between **Time series** and **Force vs. position**, and between **N** and **BW**.
- *(Optional)* Load a side-view video and mark four events to sync it with the charts.
- Work through **Check your thinking** at the bottom.

**Measure your jump height**
1. Load a side-view video of your jump. Slow motion (120–240 fps) gives the best precision.
2. Set the recorded and playback frame rates.
3. Mark **takeoff** and **landing** frame by frame to get flight time and jump height.
4. *(Optional)* Set a scale and click your COM in each airborne frame. The tool fits a parabola and compares the takeoff velocity to the flight-time estimate.

Videos stay on your device. Nothing is uploaded or saved.
