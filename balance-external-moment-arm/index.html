Balance and the External Moment Arm

Change how high the center of mass (COM) sits and how far the body leans forward. Watch the moment arm and the external moment that gravity creates about the ankle. Signs: +y anterior, +z superior, rotation about the x (mediolateral) axis, right-hand rule.

Pre-class: work alone through the guided steps, then answer the check questions before Lecture 9.

In-class: partner activity. Predict first, test it with the tool, then talk through the discussion questions.

Pre-class: guided steps

  1. Start at the default (h = 0.95 m, θ = 0.17 rad). Read d and τ, and check the arithmetic by hand.
  2. Raise θ slowly. Watch d grow, and watch the COM line approach the toe edge.
  3. Keep θ fixed and lower h. The same lean now makes a smaller moment arm and a smaller τ.
  4. Lean until the COM line passes the toe edge. That is where an ankle strategy runs out.

Check yourself

What is the moment arm d of body weight about the ankle?

The perpendicular distance from the ankle (the pivot) to the line of action of W. Here W acts straight down, so d = h·sin θ.

For a forward lean, is the external moment from gravity + or − about x?

Negative. Point your right thumb along +x (to the right): positive rotation carries +y toward +z (backward lean). Gravity pulls a forward-leaning body further forward, so τx = −W·d.

Why does crouching lower the torque for the same lean?

d = h·sin θ, so a lower COM (smaller h) gives a smaller moment arm, and τ = W·d falls with it.

What happens once the COM line passes the toe edge?

The center of pressure can't move past the edge of the foot, so the ankle muscles can no longer balance the moment. A hip strategy (moving the trunk) or a step (a new base of support) is needed.

In-class: partner challenge

  1. Predict: if h is halved at the same θ, what happens to τ? Then test: compare h = 1.00 m and 0.50 m at θ = 0.10 rad.
  2. Predict: at h = 1.00 m, which lean angle puts the COM line right over the toe edge (0.20 m)? Solve sin θ = 0.20 ÷ 1.00 first. Then test.
  3. Predict: does a heavier person have a larger moment arm, a larger torque, or both? Then test with the mass slider.

Discuss

  • For the person to stay still, what internal moment must the plantar flexors supply? How is it related to gravity's external moment, −W·d about x?
  • When would you expect someone to switch from an ankle strategy to a hip strategy, or to take a step?
  • What does this one-segment model leave out of a real crouch?

Side view (person faces right, +y anterior)

0.95 m
Height of the COM above the ankle. A standing adult's COM is roughly 0.9–1.0 m high (approximate).
0.17 rad (10°)
How far the body has leaned forward (toward +y) from true vertical. Steps of 0.005 rad (arrow keys).
70 kg
Changes W = m·g, and so τ. It does not change d.

Live calculation

Perpendicular moment arm: d = h · sin θ
d = 0.95 m × sin(0.17 rad) = 0.161 m
Moment arm (d) 0.161 m
Body weight: W = m · g
W = 70 kg × 9.81 m/s² = 686.70 N
External moment (gravity): τx = −W · d
τx = −686.70 N × 0.161 m = −110.56 N·m
External moment (τx) −110.56 N·m
COM line inside the base of support (toe edge 0.20 m ahead of the ankle).

Forward lean: gravity's external moment about x is −W·d = −110.56 N·m. To hold still, the plantar flexors supply an equal and opposite internal moment, 110.56 N·m, so the net moment is zero.

Why this matters

Body weight creates a larger external moment when the COM is higher or the lean is larger. Ankle muscles can resist only until the center of pressure reaches the edge of the foot. Beyond that, a hip strategy moves the trunk to bring the COM back over the base of support, and a step makes a new base.

Model: the body is a single-segment inverted pendulum pivoting at the ankle, with a fixed toe edge 0.20 m ahead of the ankle (an approximate adult value). "Partial crouch" only lowers h; a real crouch also bends the hip and knee, which this model ignores. Values are illustrative, not patient data.
