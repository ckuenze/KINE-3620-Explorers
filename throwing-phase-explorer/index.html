What the shoulder and elbow are doing

The amber cursor follows the animation. Curve shapes are illustrative. The only numbers that come from research are the labeled reference values, which are placed where the studies report them.

Baseball pitch

Shoulder external rotation angle Elbow varus torque Shoulder internal rotation torque
Reference values (adult pitchers). MER is about 175–180°, a combination of glenohumeral external rotation, scapular motion, and trunk extension (Dillman 1993; Fleisig 1996). From MER the arm internally rotates about 80° in the roughly 0.03 s before release, reaching about 7,000°/s (Dillman 1993). Shortly before MER: about 64 N·m elbow varus torque and 67 N·m shoulder internal rotation torque (Fleisig 1995). Not plotted: about 1090 N of shoulder compressive force shortly after release, which is what keeps the arm from being pulled out of the socket.

Windmill softball pitch

Arm position around the circle (from the animation) Shoulder force resisting distraction
Reference values. Peak compressive forces at the elbow and shoulder were 70–98% of body weight during the delivery, with shoulder extension and abduction torques of 9–10% of body weight × height (Barrentine 1998). In youth pitchers, shoulder distraction stress averaged 94% of body weight, and elbow and shoulder loads were similar to those reported for baseball pitchers (Werner 2005). Softball values are scaled to body weight, so convert before comparing them with the baseball newtons.
