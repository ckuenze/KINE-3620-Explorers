Gait Cycle Video Analyzer

Load a walking video, step through it frame by frame, mark gait events, and measure joint angles with the proximal / joint-center / distal method. Signs follow our class system: +y anterior, +z superior. Hip flexion +, knee flexion − (extension +), ankle dorsiflexion +. Angles in radians.

Pre-class: work alone through the guided steps, then answer the check questions for the reading's "Before class" list.

In-class: partner activity. Predict first, test it with the tool, then talk through the discussion questions.

Pre-class: guided steps

  1. Load a side-view (sagittal) walking clip. Enter the clip's frame rate (phone slow motion is often 120 or 240 fps).
  2. In Events & timing, step frame by frame and mark the right foot's initial contact, toe off, and the next initial contact. Read stance % from the timing box.
  3. Switch to Joint angles, pick Knee, and place hip → knee → ankle points at one frame in stance and one in swing.
  4. Move one point a little and measure again. Watch how much the angle changes.

Check yourself

At 70% of the gait cycle, is the limb in stance or swing? Which sub-phase?

Swing. Toe off is at about 60%, and initial swing runs 60–73%, so 70% is initial swing.

What do step length, stride length, and cadence each measure?

Step length: distance from one foot's contact to the other foot's contact. Stride length: one foot's contact to that same foot's next contact (two steps). Cadence: steps per minute.

Which three points do you need to calculate a joint angle?

A point on the proximal segment, the joint center, and a point on the distal segment. The angle sits at the joint center.

Why is each joint center an estimate?

None of them is visible from outside. The hip center is the femoral head inside the pelvis, the knee center sits between femur and tibia, and the ankle center is the talocrural joint. On video you place a skin landmark that stands in for each one.

Joint angle vs ROM vs joint excursion?

Joint angle: the angle at one instant. ROM: the total motion available at the joint, usually measured passively in a clinic. Excursion: the part of that range actually used during a task such as walking.

In-class: partner challenge

  1. Predict: what stance % will your partner's walk show? Then test: film 2–3 strides from the side, mark initial contact, toe off, and the next initial contact, and read stance %.
  2. Predict: is the knee near 0 rad or clearly flexed (−) at initial contact? At mid swing? Then test: measure both frames.
  3. Predict: how does cadence change when your partner walks fast? Then test: mark opposite initial contact to get step time and cadence for both speeds.
  4. Load a front-view clip too, sync the two, and measure step width (|Δx|). Set a scale with a known length to report metres.

Discuss

  • What happens to the knee sign if the walking direction setting is wrong? Why?
  • How would a camera that is not perpendicular to the walking path change your sagittal angles?
  • Your partner and you placed the knee point on the same frame. Why might your angles differ?
