Muscle Architecture Explorer

Two design choices in a muscle: how steeply the fibers sit (pennation angle α) and how long the fascicles are. α is measured from the tendon’s line of action, not from the bone. Angles are in radians.

Pre-class: work alone through the guided steps, then answer the check questions for the reading's "Before class" list.

In-class: partner activity. Predict first, test it with the tool, then talk through the discussion questions.

Pre-class: guided steps

  1. Set α to 0 rad. All of the fiber force acts along the tendon.
  2. Raise α to 0.26 rad, then to 0.70 rad. Watch how much of the fiber force reaches the tendon, and read Lost to angle.
  3. In Window 2, press Play. Fascicle A is 0.10 m and fascicle B is 0.05 m, with sarcomeres shortening at the same rate. Which fascicle shortens faster in m/s?

Check yourself

A fiber pulls with 1000 N at α = 0.26 rad. What force acts along the tendon?

F·cos α = 1000 N × 0.96639 = 966.39 N. This is the same “parallel component” idea as angle of pull in Lecture 7, but α is measured from the tendon line.

If cos α is less than 1, how can a more pennate muscle still be stronger?

Angled fibers pack more fibers side by side in the same volume, so physiologic cross-sectional area (PCSA) goes up. More fibers in parallel means more total force, even though each one loses a little to cos α. Past some angle, the loss is no longer made up.

Which muscle is built more for speed, vastus lateralis or medial gastrocnemius?

Vastus lateralis. Its fascicles are longer (more sarcomeres in series), so the fascicle can shorten faster in m/s. The medial gastrocnemius is more pennate with shorter fascicles, which favors force over speed.

In-class: partner challenge

  1. Predict: at what α does the tendon get only 90% of the fiber force? Then test: find it with the slider (watch “lost to angle”).
  2. Predict: fascicle A (0.10 m) is twice as long as B (0.05 m). How much faster does A shorten? Then test: press Play, then drag B up to 0.10 m (A stays 0.10 m).
  3. Predict: to match B’s speed in m/s, how slowly must A’s sarcomeres shorten? Then test: change the sarcomere rate and compare.

Discuss

  • Pennation trades a little force per fiber for many more fibers. Where would that trade stop paying off?
  • For the same fascicle speed, why does a longer fascicle sit higher on the force–velocity curve?
  • Which architecture would you want in a muscle used for sprinting? For holding a heavy load?
